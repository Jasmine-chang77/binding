Introduction

Typography is often described as a set of rules: grids, measures, classifications. It is also a gesture: the hand, the body, the shape a word makes before we read it. Sorts brings together three texts from either side of that tension. Ellen Lupton’s Thinking with Type traces typography from calligraphy to code. Bruno Munari's The Shape of Words shows what happens when words take on shapes of their own. Anthony Froshaug's Typography is a Grid argues that typography is a grid, built into the rectangular metal of type itself.

This reader is built the way type was once set. Every element is a sort, a single block fitted into a frame, and every empty cell is spacing material, holding the others in place.
